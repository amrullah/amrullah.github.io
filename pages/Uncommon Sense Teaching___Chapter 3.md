- # Key Ideas of this Chapter
    - A commonly understood definition of [[active learning]] is that it engages students in the process of learning through activities and/or discussion in class. It frequently involves some form of [[retrieval practice]] as opposed to passively listening to an expert. [[Active Learning]] emphasizes higher-order thinking and often involves partner or group work.
    - From a [[neuroscientific]] perspective, [[Active Learning]] supports the creation and [[Consolidation]] of [[neural]] links in [[LTM]] that underlie both, a rudimentary and a higher-order conceptual understanding of the. material. It is often part of the *link it* phase of [[learn it, link it]]. Not all [[Learning]] is [[Active Learning]].
    - The [[declarative learning pathway]] sends information from [[WM]] to both, the [[hippocampus]] (indexing the information) and the [[neocortex]] (storing the information in the [[LTM]] )
    - When the [[hippocampus]] has a break in the incoming stream of information from [[WM]], it can turn to the [[neocortex]] and reinforce it's learning by telling it over and over again which links to strengthen and which to weaken.
    - Brain breaks occur naturally while students transition from a collaborative exercise such as [[think-pair-share]] or when they do low-key thinking about the materials on their own. Just as athletics requires strenuous physical activity coupled with rest, [[learning]] develops through hard mental work paired with mental rest.
    - Encourage [[retrieval practice]], which speeds [[consolidation]] of links in the [[neocortex]]. [[Retrieval Practice]] allows the information stored in [[LTM]] to be accessed more directly by [[WM]] without going through the [[hippocampus]]

    - ## Two important ways of remembering
      collapsed:: true
        - ### [[Declarative memory]]
            - Involves facts and events that can be consciously ^^recalled^^, or *declared*.
            - The [[declarative memory]] system relates to [[Working Memory]], the [[Hippocampus]] and [[Long Term Memory]] in the [[Neocortex]]
        - ### [[Procedural memory]]
            - Often involves how to do something, such as typing on a keyboard, tying a shoelace or steps to solving a math problem.
            - The [[procedural memory]] system involves the [[Basal Ganglia]] as well as the [[Neocortex]]
        - When [[declarative memory]] and [[procedural memory]] systems can [[learn]] the same [[Concept]] in two different ways, they provide a richer way for students to understand the material.

- # Using the [[Declarative system]] to get Information into [[Long Term Memory]]
    - We can think of [[Working Memory]] as a conductor of a tiny choir of two singers, the [[Hippocampus]] and the [[Neocortex]].
      collapsed:: true
        - ![Working Memory Choir](..assets/uncommon_sense_learning_illustrations/Fig_13_pg_47_brain_like_a_choir.png)

    - The [[Hippocampus]] can [[learn]] swiftly, but only the most superficial, indexing aspects of the information. The [[Neocortex]] on the other hand, because it has massive number of [[neural]] links to connect learns much more slowly and with great difficulty. But it can [[learn]] a *lot*, and very well.
      collapsed:: true
        - ![Neocortex's mastery](..assets/uncommon_sense_learning_illustrations/Fig_14_pg_48_neocortex_sings_loudly.png)
    - ## Mechanism
      id:: 68fb29f9-55b8-4097-8809-b014e2d4b07c
        - The [[Working Memory]] deposits sets of ([[neural]]) links in [[Long Term Memory]] ([[neocortex]]) about what is being [[learned]]. Simultaneously, the [[Working Memory]] deposits indexing information in the [[Hippocampus]] about where to find those links.
        - Whenever [[Hippocampus]] isn't busy taking in new indexing information, it reverberates the [[Ingested]] information to [[Neocortex]], which has information scattered around in many places. This offline teaching by the [[Hippocampus]] to the [[Neocortex]] is an essential aspect of [[declarative learning]].
            - Much of it takes place during sleep.
        - In effect, The [[Hippocampus]] is the primary teacher in [[declarative learning]] because the [[Neocortex]] isn't quick enough to follow [[Working Memory]] in real time.
        - [[Hippocampus]] whispers over and over again to [[Neocortex]] helping it know which [[neural]] links to strengthen and which ones to weaken. It can take many days, weeks and months for this [[hippocampal]] shaping of the links in the [[Neocortex]] to take place. This process is called [[Consolidation]]
          collapsed:: true
            - ![Hippocampus roles](..assets/uncommon_sense_learning_illustrations/Fig_15_pg_50_hippocampus_two_positions.png)
            -
        - ### What happens when [[WM]] is trying to retrieve the information from [[Hippocampus]] and Neocortex? #[[Retrieval Practice]]
            - If [[Working Memory]] calls for recently [[learned]] information, the [[Neocortex]]'s grip on the information is weak, so [[Hippocampus]] chimes in. Reminding [[Neocortex]] where all the bits of information are located.
            - But once the [[Hippocampus]] and the [[Neocortex]] have worked together enough number of times on a material, [[Hippocampus]]' help is less necessary. The [[Neocortex]] becomes master of the material, despite the relative complexity of the information it holds.
                - That's the clue something is well [[learned]].
            - The better a student knows the material, the more their [[Working Memory]] can reach directly into the [[Neocortex]] to get it, without the need of [[Hippocampus]].
    - ## Helping [[Hippocampus]] in the classroom with small breaks in the teaching
        - One valuable insight from the ((68fb29f9-55b8-4097-8809-b014e2d4b07c)) is that it is important to have brief "[[Brain]] breaks" in your teaching, where students have a chance to relax mentally.
        - Our brains take an eight-hour break at night when we fall asleep, when memories are consolidated on a global scale.
          collapsed:: true
            - Sleep is when your [[Brain]] goes offline for repair and large-scale reorganization. Constructing new [[synapses]] and enlarging others requires [[proteins]] and other building materials to be synthesized. This involves biochemical reactions that build the cytoskeleton scaffolding and enormous macromolecular complexes at [[synapses]].
        - But much of the preparation is done during briefer breaks throughout the day.
        - One [study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30683602/) revealed that 15 minute period of eyes closed rest following [[Learning]] enhanced the memory of what was just [[learned]] far more than when the participants simply went on to another task.
            - But even a pause of less than a minute can work wonders in enabling students to begin making better sense of the material, as it gives [[Neurons]] a chance to consolidate #Consolidation
        - When you go on presenting information for too long, you can almost feel the gradual tension, coupled with boredom, begin to arise.
            - A good rule of thumb is that students attention span will last about their age + 1.
                - For example, a 7 year old may be able to focus for 8 minutes on an intense activity before needing a short break.

- # Key Ideas of this Chapter
    - [[Learning]] involves connecting, strengthening and extending sets of neural links in the [[Long Term Memory]]. It is referred to in this book as [[Learn it, link it]]
    - The strengthening of connections between links as a student practices is known as [[Hebbian Learning]]
    - There are many types of memory with different purposes. Two of the most important types of Memory for classroom [[Learning]] are [[Working Memory]] and [[long-term memory]].
        - ^^Information in [[WM]] can fade away within seconds^^.
        - Information in [[LTM]] is more ^^persistent^^, sometimes lasting (with subtle and occasionally not-so-subtle shifts) for a lifetime.
    - An average [[WM]] can hold upto ^^four^^ "balls" of information before the ideas begin ^^slipping^^ from the mind.
    - Students will often place information in the [[WM]] and mistakenly believe that it has made it's way into [[LTM]]. Later they can perform poorly on tests because they have no information to draw from [[LTM]]
    - [[Retrieval Practice]] encourages and strengthens the connections between [[neurons]] in [[LTM]] and prevents students' [[WM]] from tricking them.

            - Once a [[Neuron]] involved in the [[Learning]] process comes close enough to a ^^neighboring^^ [[Neuron]], a signal can jump across the narrow gap (called [[synapse]] )
- # [[Learn it, link it]]
    - As students are [[Learning]], the [[Neurons]] are linking and strengthening.
        - The [[Authors]] refer to this process as [[Learn it, link it]]
        - This [[Term]] has it's origins in [[Hebbian Learning]], a process where [[Neurons]] fire together near simultaneously, in time wire together.
            - Named after Donald Hebb
        - When a certain set of [[Neurons]] begins to work together more often, they become like a well-practiced choir.

- # [[Long Term Memory]] versus [[Working Memory]]
    - ## [[Long Term Memory]]
        - It holds the information we're able to store and bring back to mind from weeks, months or even years before.
        - You can think of [[LTM]] as sets of [[neural]] links that one has developed when they've [[learned]] the material well.
            - These [[neural]] links congregate in the [[neocortex]].
        - If we reinforce the links of long-term [[Learning]] through varied practice, our [[Learning]] is generally in good shape.
            - Varied practice here means applying or trying to recall the knowledge in varying contexts.
    - ## [[Working Memory]]
        - The [[Authors]] used the analogy of an *octopus tossing a set of balls* to explain this [[Concept]]. These balls represent the *thoughts that bounce* over and over agin from the front to the back of your brain.
        - An average [[WM]] can hold upto **four** "balls" before the ideas begin slipping from the mind.
            - Humans can't grow more slots to hold more than four ideas, but more the students practice with the material, bigger the balls of information can become.
        - A quirky thing about [[WM]] is that whenever the octopus gets *distracted* from throwing and catching balls, the balls can *vanish*.
            - And this fact contributes to it's #WM cunning ability to *fool students* into thinking surely *they've put something in the [[LTM]]*
                - This ephemeral nature of [[WM]] surfaces in situations like these:
                    - A student can stare at a list of 10 new vocabulary words and think, *I've got them!* But they aren't going to stay that long and the student won't be in a position to use them in a conversation.
                    - A student glances at the solution to a complex math problem, but cannot solve the problems of this kind in a test.
                    - Even in my [[personal experience]], in 12th Boards exam, I was not able to answer a question from Phenols section in Organic Chemistry. That was the only part that I hadn't repetitively practiced well and had relied on last minute glancing.
                - Students often can't tell whether they have something in their [[LTM]] or in [[WM]]
    - ## [[Retrieval Practice]]
        - Getting information in [[LTM]] can be difficult. However [[retrieval practice]] is one of the best techniques for strengthening new information in [[LTM]].
            - Refer: ((68f61743-3b29-4d07-81fc-131c6a85c5b4))
        - Good Examples of [[retrieval practice]] include using flash cards or merely looking away from page to see if you can retrieve the key idea or ideas on the page.
        - It is far from simple mindless memorization, it also builds conceptual understanding. It is about *drawing ideas* you have learnt, *from your own mind*, rather than simply looking at the answer.

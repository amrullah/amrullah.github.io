- Today's large-scale Software Systems are among the most complex structures ever built by humans, containing millions of lines of code, thousands of database tables, hundreds of components, all running on dozens of computers. 
  
  This presents some formidable challenges to software development teams -- and if these challenges aren't addressed early, systems are delivered late, over budget, or with an unacceptably poor level of quality.
- # Stakeholders, Viewpoints and Perspectives
    - This book focuses on three fundamental concepts: [[stakeholders]] ,  [[viewpoints]] and [[perspectives]] .
    - Traditionally, Software Developers have concentrated on the needs of end users and sometimes developers, but this is far too narrow a view if you want to create a system that satisfies everyone affected by it.
    - The people affected by our system are called [[stakeholders]] and because their needs is why the system is being created in the first place, meeting these needs is your chief goal as a [[Software Architect]]
    - ^^What^^ a system does is only part of the story and ^^How^^ the system provides it's services often has a huge impact on the perceptions that [[stakeholders]] have of it. The architecture you ^^choose^^ for your system dictates how ^^quickly^^ it runs, how ^^secure^^ it is, how ^^available^^ it is, how ^^easy to modify^^ it is and many other ^^non-functional^^ factors, which are collectively termed as [[quality properties]].
    - [[Stakeholders]] are the people for whom we build systems. A key part of your role as a [[Software Architect]] is knowing how to work with stakeholders in order to create an architecture that meets their complex, overlapping and often conflicting needs
    - [[Viewpoints]] (and [[Views]] ) are an approach to structuring the architecture definition process and the architectural description, based on the principle of [[Separation of Concerns]]. Viewpoints contain proven architectural knowledge to guide the creation of an architecture, described in a particular set of views
    - [[Perspectives]] are a complimentary concept to [[viewpoints]] that contain proven architectural knowledge and help structure the architecture definition process by separating the concerns, but focussing on cross-structural [[quality properties]] rather than architectural structures.
    - [[Architecture definition]] is not a linear flow that can be easily written down, but in reality it is an incremental process involving an iterative cycle of information capture, model development, review and refinement.
